The short version ChainKit has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no cookies. I do not run a server that could receive your data, and I never see what you look up.

But ChainKit is not magic. To find a transaction, whatever you paste has to be sent to the public blockchain services that hold that data — and those are other people's servers. This page explains exactly which ones, and what that means for you.

That summary is a guide, not the agreement. Where it differs from the sections below, the sections govern.

01 Who is responsible

ChainKit is run by Hunter Rodriguez, an individual, as a personal project. In data protection language I am the controller for the limited processing described here. Contact details are in section 17.

This policy covers this website only. It does not cover the third-party services ChainKit links to or queries — those are run by other people under their own policies.

02 How ChainKit is built

Understanding the architecture explains most of this policy, so it is worth one paragraph.

ChainKit is a single static web page. There is no application server, no database, and no backend of mine anywhere. When you use it, the page is delivered to your browser and then all of the work happens inside your browser, on your own device. Your browser talks directly to public blockchain services. Nothing routes through me, and there is no point at which I could collect, read, or store what you searched for — even if I wanted to.

What ChainKit does not have
  • No user accounts, logins, or profiles
  • No analytics of any kind — no Google Analytics, no Plausible, no page-view counter, nothing
  • No advertising, no advertising identifiers, and no data sold or shared with brokers
  • No tracking pixels, beacons, fingerprinting, or session recording
  • No cookies
  • No newsletter, no mailing list, no contact form
  • No access to your wallet's private keys or seed phrase, ever
I have no plans to add any of these. If that ever changed, this policy would change first.

Fonts and icons are served from this site's own domain rather than from Google Fonts or a content delivery network, specifically so that loading the page does not hand your IP address to a third party.

03 What stays on your device

ChainKit stores a small amount of information in your browser's local storage. This never leaves your device and is not transmitted anywhere. It is not a cookie and is not sent with any request.

chainkit_themeWhether you chose light, dark or automatic colours.
chainkit_tzWhether you chose your local time, UTC, or Panama time.
chainkit_historyYour last 15 lookups, so the Lookup tab can offer them again. This may contain wallet addresses and transaction hashes you searched for.

You are in complete control of this. The Lookup tab has a Clear button that deletes the history immediately. Clearing your browser's site data removes everything. Using private or incognito browsing means nothing persists after you close the window. If your browser blocks local storage, ChainKit falls back to holding these values in memory only, and they vanish when you close the tab.

Anyone with physical access to your device can read this, the same as your browser history. On a shared or public computer, clear it when you finish.

04 What is sent to third parties

This is the part that matters most, so it is set out plainly.

ChainKit cannot answer a question about a blockchain without asking the people who hold that data. When you search, your browser sends what you typed directly to one or more public blockchain services. Those services can see the value you searched for and, as with any web request, your IP address and browser user-agent.

This is unavoidable, not a design choice The blockchain data lives on their servers, not mine. Any tool that looks up a transaction must contact somebody who has it — including every block explorer you have ever used. The difference is that ChainKit tells you exactly who, which most do not.

05 Why wallet addresses count as personal data

It would be convenient to claim that a wallet address is anonymous. That is not accurate, and this policy is not going to pretend otherwise.

A wallet address is a persistent identifier. On its own it names no one, but combined with other information — an exchange's records, a public post, a transaction with someone who knows you, or an IP address recorded at the moment of the lookup — it can be traced back to a person. Under the GDPR and similar laws, that makes it pseudonymous personal data rather than anonymous data, and it is treated as personal data here.

The practical consequences:

  • Searching your own address means transmitting a piece of your personal data to third parties. You should know that before you do it, which is why it is stated plainly.
  • Searching someone else's address means transmitting information about them. Blockchains are public and looking at them is lawful, but if you are compiling information about a specific private individual, you may have your own legal obligations. That is on you, not on ChainKit.
  • ChainKit's own lookup history is stored on your device only, so I never hold any of it.

06 Web hosting and server logs

ChainKit is hosted on GitHub Pages, a service of GitHub, Inc. (a Microsoft company). Delivering a web page requires a server, and that server records requests.

GitHub collects standard technical information when your browser requests this page, which typically includes your IP address, the time of the request, the page requested, and your user-agent string. GitHub states it uses this for security, abuse prevention and service operation.

An honest limitation These are GitHub's logs, not mine. I cannot see them, cannot export them, and cannot delete them. If you ask me to erase everything about you, I can confirm that I hold nothing — but I cannot reach into GitHub's infrastructure on your behalf. For anything relating to those logs you would need to approach GitHub directly, under their privacy statement.

I have no analytics account attached to this site and receive no visitor reports, dashboards, or statistics of any kind.

07 Cookies and similar technology

ChainKit sets no cookies. There is no cookie banner because there is nothing to consent to.

The local storage described in section 3 is technically a "similar technology" under the EU ePrivacy rules. It is used only to remember settings you chose and lookups you performed — never to track you, build a profile, or identify you across sites, and it is never transmitted. Your theme and timezone choices are strictly necessary to provide the display you asked for. Your lookup history exists purely as a convenience to you, is visible on the page, and can be deleted with one click.

Nothing is shared with any third party, and no advertising or measurement technology is present.

08 Legal bases for processing

For visitors covered by the UK or EU GDPR, or similar regimes:

  • Performing the service you requested. Sending your search to blockchain services is the only way to answer your question, and you initiate it deliberately by pressing Search. Legal basis: legitimate interests, and where relevant, the necessity of taking steps at your request.
  • Keeping the site available and secure. Server logs held by the host serve a legitimate interest in operating the site and preventing abuse.
  • Storing your preferences on your device. Necessary to provide the functionality you asked for.
  • Correspondence. If you email me, I process what you send in order to reply.

I do not rely on consent for anything, because there is nothing optional being collected. I do not carry out profiling, and I make no automated decisions that produce legal or similarly significant effects.

10 International transfers

ChainKit is available worldwide and the services it relies on are distributed globally. When your browser contacts a blockchain node or an explorer API, that request may be handled anywhere in the world, including outside your own country and outside the EEA or UK.

These transfers happen directly between your browser and those services. I am not an intermediary, do not route the traffic, and am not in a position to impose contractual transfer safeguards on services I have no relationship with. This is inherent to how a browser-based blockchain tool works, and it is why it is described here rather than glossed over.

If where your data is handled matters to you, use a VPN, or use a blockchain explorer you have vetted yourself instead of this tool.

14 Security

Because there is no database and no server of mine, the usual risk — someone breaching a company and stealing user records — does not apply. There are no records to steal.

The measures that do apply:

  • The site is served only over HTTPS, so traffic between your browser and the host is encrypted.
  • A strict content security policy limits what the page may load and which servers it may contact, which reduces the damage any injected code could do.
  • Fonts and icons are self-hosted, so no third-party asset provider sees your visit.
  • The full source is published, so anyone can audit these claims rather than trust them.
  • Outbound links use protections that prevent the destination page from manipulating the tab it was opened from.

No system is perfectly secure. If you find a vulnerability, please report it privately to the address below rather than publicly, and give me a reasonable chance to fix it.

15 Things you should be careful about

Never enter a seed phrase or private key — anywhere ChainKit will never ask for one, and has no field that could accept one. No legitimate service ever will. Anyone asking is trying to steal from you. A wallet address is public and safe to paste; a seed phrase is the key to everything you own.

Shared links contain what you searched. The Copy link button produces a URL with your search term in it. If you send that to someone, they can see the address or hash. That is usually the point, but be deliberate about it.

Your history is visible to anyone using your device. On a shared or public computer, press Clear before you walk away.

Connecting a wallet reveals your address to ChainKit's page. That happens in your browser and is not transmitted to me, but your wallet extension will show you what is being requested. You never have to connect one: every donation address is displayed in full so you can copy it instead.

Third-party sites have their own rules. Once you click a Directory link you are on somebody else's site, subject to their policy, not this one.
